SYNTHETIC NOTE FOR RESEARCH. NOT A REAL PATIENT.

A 62-year-old man in the emergency department has 3 days of progressive shortness of breath, right-sided pleuritic chest pain, and a dry cough. He has a temperature of 37.0°C, a heart rate of 108 beats/min, an oxygen saturation of 96% on room air, and clear breath sounds bilaterally. He has no hemoptysis, no history of cancer, no recent surgery or immobilization, no prior DVT or PE, and no leg swelling or calf tenderness. The clinician judges pulmonary embolism to be the leading explanation, with pneumonia and pleurisy less likely. A CT pulmonary angiogram shows no filling defect in the pulmonary arteries.
